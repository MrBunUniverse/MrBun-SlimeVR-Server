# Your Mac Can Receive Quest Tracking

**Yes: your Quest 3S can supply its real headset position and rotation to your custom SlimeVR server on the M3 MacBook Air, without a Windows PC.** The clearest existing route uses standalone VRChat's headset tracking output, and your checked-out server already contains its receiver. It does not require Valve SteamVR. Running actual Valve SteamVR on this Mac is a separate problem: no supported or verified complete Quest 3S, SteamVR and SlimeVR driver setup was established by this research. Experimental Mac PCVR alternatives exist, but accepting a headset pose and supplying full-body trackers to a PCVR game are separate integration tasks. A capable coding model can help build a missing bridge; the immediate next step is to test the bridge you already have. These conclusions combine current primary documentation with your local source, not a live headset test.

## Your server already accepts real Quest headset movement

VRChat officially introduced outgoing headset and wrist tracking from the VR system in release 2023.3.1. Applications receiving it must use OSCQuery, and the user must enable **Allow Sending Head and Wrist VR Tracking OSC Data**. The documented outgoing head address is `/tracking/vrsystem/head/pose`, carrying position and orientation. SlimeVR's official standalone guide explicitly includes Quest 3S and permits SlimeVR to run on another laptop or computer. This provides a documented Mac-plus-Quest route for your intended head anchor. ([VRChat release notes](https://docs.vrchat.com/docs/vrchat-202331), [VRChat OSC reference](https://wiki.vrchat.com/wiki/OSC), [SlimeVR standalone setup](https://docs.slimevr.dev/server/osc-information.html))

Your fork subscribes to that exact head endpoint. On its first head packet, it creates a tracker named **VRChat head**, assigns it to Head, declares position and rotation support, converts coordinates, and updates its tracking status. The skeleton consumes the selected head tracker's position and rotation. This is source evidence for receiving actual headset tracking, rather than estimating head motion from an avatar. It does not prove that the currently installed app matches this checkout or that packets are arriving on your network. ([Head receiver](</Users/sirjame/Documents/Project/SJ SlimeVR Sever Claude/server/core/src/main/java/dev/slimevr/osc/VRCOSCHandler.kt:386>), [Skeleton head input](</Users/sirjame/Documents/Project/SJ SlimeVR Sever Claude/server/core/src/main/java/dev/slimevr/tracking/processor/skeleton/HumanSkeleton.kt:778>))

The similar address `/tracking/trackers/head/position` has a different purpose: it is input **to VRChat** for aligning incoming trackers with the avatar's head bone. Reading or generating that value does not retrieve the headset's measured pose. The outgoing `/tracking/vrsystem/head/pose` is the relevant source. Its six floats represent XYZ position and Euler rotation, not a quaternion. Its coordinate frame also needs practical checks during recentering, locomotion and avatar scaling; the documentation does not establish an immutable raw sensor origin. ([VRChat tracker input documentation](https://docs.vrchat.com/docs/osc-trackers), [VRChat OSC reference](https://wiki.vrchat.com/wiki/OSC))

## Enable standalone OSC before attempting a new integration

In your custom app, open the top-bar connection/status control and turn on **Standalone mode (VRChat OSC)**. Then open **Settings → OSC → VRChat OSC Trackers**, and confirm both **Enable** and **Enable OSCQuery** are on. Check the explicit settings even if the badge already says Standalone: the GUI's saved mode preference alone does not prove the server's saved receiver setting. Your current mode switch enables OSC for standalone and disables it for PC VR, so selecting PC VR would switch off the route needed here. ([Status control](</Users/sirjame/Documents/Project/SJ SlimeVR Sever Claude/gui/src/components/shell/StatusCluster.tsx:199>), [Mode behavior](</Users/sirjame/Documents/Project/SJ SlimeVR Sever Claude/gui/src/hooks/operating-mode.ts:98>), [OSC settings](</Users/sirjame/Documents/Project/SJ SlimeVR Sever Claude/gui/src/components/settings/pages/VRCOSCSettings.tsx:196>))

On the Quest, run standalone VRChat. Enable OSC through its Action/Radial Menu under **Options → OSC**, then enable **Allow Sending Head and Wrist VR Tracking OSC Data** in the main settings under **Tracking & IK**. Keep the Mac and Quest on the same reachable local network. SlimeVR's outgoing tracker configuration should target the Quest's LAN address when the server runs on the Mac; that outbound address does not replace OSCQuery discovery for receiving headset data. ([SlimeVR setup guide](https://docs.slimevr.dev/server/osc-information.html), [VRChat head-output requirement](https://docs.vrchat.com/docs/vrchat-202331))

The first acceptance check is a **VRChat head** tracker appearing and responding correctly when you turn, lean, move sideways and crouch. Then check its behavior after recentering and reconnecting before relying on it for body calibration. Your server advertises its OSCQuery service and listens for VRChat discovery automatically; a manual scan is not the prerequisite. If no head arrives, investigate discovery, the selected network interface, the VRChat privacy toggle and incoming OSC packets. Changing the outbound Quest IP alone cannot establish that reverse path. Network filtering and client isolation are diagnostic possibilities, not confirmed faults in your setup. ([Discovery implementation](</Users/sirjame/Documents/Project/SJ SlimeVR Sever Claude/server/core/src/main/java/dev/slimevr/osc/VRCOSCQueryHandler.kt:29>), [Receiver enablement](</Users/sirjame/Documents/Project/SJ SlimeVR Sever Claude/server/core/src/main/java/dev/slimevr/osc/VRCOSCHandler.kt:114>))

## Valve SteamVR and experimental Mac PCVR are different targets

Valve ended SteamVR support for macOS in 2020. Its OpenVR repository is an SDK whose runtime is provided separately by SteamVR; compiling OpenVR bindings does not supply the missing working runtime. Upstream ALVR supports the Quest 3S client but does not support a macOS server. Your own SlimeVR desktop entry point creates native SteamVR bridges for Windows and Linux, with no corresponding macOS branch. Together, these explain why a familiar SteamVR headset entry cannot simply be enabled on your Mac. ([Valve announcement](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/250820/view/2216278054495230717), [OpenVR SDK](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/openvr/blob/master/README.md), [ALVR platforms](https://github.com/alvr-org/ALVR), [Your platform bridge setup](</Users/sirjame/Documents/Project/SJ SlimeVR Sever Claude/server/desktop/src/main/java/dev/slimevr/desktop/Main.kt:199>))

**Mac PCVR is nevertheless an active experimental engineering path.** MoltenVR publishes Apple Silicon builds that combine Wine, graphics translation, a custom OpenXR runtime and an ALVR headset client. Its project claims full headset/controller 6DoF and selected working games, and explicitly distinguishes itself from SteamVR for Mac. BasaltVR publishes an open-source developer preview using a similar replacement-runtime approach. These are actual downloadable projects, not proof of universal compatibility. ([MoltenVR project](https://github.com/MoltenVR/MoltenVR-Preview), [MoltenVR v1.0.2](https://github.com/MoltenVR/MoltenVR-Preview/releases/tag/v1.0.2), [BasaltVR developer release](https://github.com/frenchbaguetteman/BasaltVR/releases/tag/v0.1.0))

Basalt records testing on an M3 Max with Quest 3 and describes its runtime as experimental and non-conformant. That does not establish acceptable performance or compatibility on your M3 MacBook Air with Quest 3S. More critically, a replacement runtime does not automatically load SlimeVR's existing SteamVR driver. Getting head pose into SlimeVR and exposing SlimeVR's solved body poses to a game require separate proof. No verified plug-and-play SlimeVR full-body integration for these Mac runtimes was found in the reviewed primary material. ([BasaltVR scope](https://github.com/frenchbaguetteman/BasaltVR), [Basalt runtime](https://github.com/frenchbaguetteman/BasaltVR/tree/main/runtime))

## A coding model can build a bounded bridge

For use outside standalone VRChat, the most credible AI-assisted project is a **small head-pose exporter from an existing runtime**. Basalt's documented architecture already receives Quest tracking through ALVR, normalizes head orientation and forwards pose to its OpenXR runtime while handling freshness and reference spaces. An exporter at that existing boundary could feed an appropriate SlimeVR input. This is an engineering inference from documented components; the exact source hook and working end-to-end behavior still require implementation and hardware validation. MoltenVR's binary-only distribution makes equivalent changes dependent on an available interface or maintainer cooperation. ([Basalt architecture](https://github.com/frenchbaguetteman/BasaltVR/blob/main/docs/ARCHITECTURE.md), [Basalt runtime](https://github.com/frenchbaguetteman/BasaltVR/tree/main/runtime), [MoltenVR distribution](https://github.com/MoltenVR/MoltenVR-Preview))

Your fork offers reusable inputs, but they carry assumptions. Its WebSocket HMD route adds 0.2 metres to incoming Y, while the VMC HMD path does not automatically assign Head. Simply sending SlimeVR UDP position packet 27 is also insufficient to establish a working positional HMD tracker. A model should inspect and adapt one appropriate existing route, then verify axes, metres, origin/recenter alignment, valid tracking and stale-packet expiry. Reusing the existing VRChat route remains the smallest solution for VRChat itself. ([WebSocket pose parser](</Users/sirjame/Documents/Project/SJ SlimeVR Sever Claude/server/core/src/main/java/dev/slimevr/websocketapi/WebSocketVRBridge.kt:162>), [VMC HMD input](</Users/sirjame/Documents/Project/SJ SlimeVR Sever Claude/server/core/src/main/java/dev/slimevr/osc/VMCHandler.kt:222>))

A standalone Quest OpenXR sender is another possible prototype: read pose while its own XR session runs and transmit it to the Mac. Meta supplies OpenXR development APIs and samples. However, those sources do not prove that such a companion can keep obtaining full headset pose alongside every other immersive Quest app. ADB connectivity and scrcpy mirroring do not themselves provide that runtime pose feed. ([Meta OpenXR background](https://developers.meta.com/vr/documentation/unity/os-openxr-vrapi/), [Meta native samples](https://developers.meta.com/vr/documentation/native/native-openxr-sdk-sample/), [Meta session lifecycle](https://developers.meta.com/vr/documentation/native/android/mobile-openxr-instance-session/), [scrcpy capabilities](https://github.com/Genymobile/scrcpy))

The proposed model name “Opus 5.5” is not verified here. A capable coding model can help implement and debug these defined integrations, but model size cannot establish unavailable runtime support or substitute for real headset measurements. Full Mac PCVR with general SlimeVR body-tracker injection is a larger project than showing a genuine head tracker in your app.

## Conclusion

Start by proving receipt of the existing VRChat system-head feed on your actual Quest and Mac. That result decides whether the immediate work is configuration, discovery diagnostics or a source fix, and avoids rebuilding an input your server already supports. If your goal expands to other Mac PCVR games, define one runtime and one game first; a verified head exporter is a useful milestone, while full-body game integration needs its own acceptance test.
